1 · A home screen (recommended)

Opening the app lands here: a streak seal, today's five-minute plan, and a live preview of the 180-move map. はじめる starts the session full-screen, and ✕ brings you back here. Progress and Settings open from the two icons.

+ The progress map is the app's only source of motivation (per the spec), and it's the first thing you see each day. The timer starts only when the learner taps はじめる.
− Starting a session takes one extra tap. ✕ mid-session needs a decision about what happens to that session.

2 · Bottom tab bar

The standard iOS pattern: 今日 / 進捗 / 設定 as tabs. The session runs inside the 今日 tab.

+ Familiar, and every screen is always one tap away.
− The tab bar takes space from the soroban and keypad (note how cramped it is). Being able to switch tabs mid-question works against a fixed, focused five minutes.

3 · Straight into the session (the current flow, restyled)

Opening the app starts practice right away, as it does now. Progress and Settings become two small icons on either side of the session's progress bar.

+ Nothing stands between opening the app and practising, and it's the smallest change.
− The streak and the map only show if you go looking for them. The clock is already running while the learner settles in, and it's easy to tap the icons mid-session.

Map colours in style A get darker as a move is learned, like wood deepening under lacquer: unseen (pale paper) → learning (ochre) → reflex (boxwood) → mental (walnut). Because only the lightness changes, colour-blind learners can read it too. The Japanese labels here (準備・集中・暗算・まとめ, 今日の五分) are placeholder copy.
