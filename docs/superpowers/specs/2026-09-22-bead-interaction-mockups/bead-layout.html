A · The soroban takes the keypad's place (recommended)

At F0–F2 the keypad is gone, and the soroban grows by about 40% and moves down into thumb reach. The prompt and demonstration sit above it. もどす puts the beads back to the starting number, and こたえる reads the soroban.

Why: beads at the normal size are 50×21pt, well under Apple's 44pt minimum tap target. At this size they're 69×29pt, and the rods are 88pt wide. The soroban is the instrument now, so it gets the space.

B · Same layout, keypad swapped for two buttons

The soroban stays at the top at today's size, so the screen matches the typed levels exactly. Only the bottom changes.

Cost: small tap targets at the top of the screen, far from the thumb, with a large empty area in the middle.

What a tap does (both layouts), as on a real soroban: tap a bead that's away from the beam and it moves to the beam, pushing every bead between it and the beam along with it. Tap a bead that's at the beam and it moves back, taking every bead beyond it too. The heaven bead toggles. Beads slide into place over a short animation rather than jumping.

What stays the same: at F3 and above (beads dimmed, ghosted or gone), the screen is today's typed layout. The number pad is for working in your head. The reading drill stays typed too, since it teaches reading beads, not moving them. There's no live number readout for the beads, because showing one would do the reading for the learner.
